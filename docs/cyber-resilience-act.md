# The Cyber Resilience Act: what this covers, and what it does not

Since 11 September 2026, a manufacturer that sells software in the European Union has 24 hours to warn its national CSIRT and ENISA once it learns that a vulnerability in its product is being actively exploited. The rest of the Cyber Resilience Act applies from 11 December 2027. Most of what the regulation asks of software is evidence about the supply chain, and that evidence has to exist before an auditor asks for it.

This page maps the controls in this repository to the clauses of [Regulation (EU) 2024/2847](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj/eng) they produce evidence for. Every quotation is from the text published in the Official Journal on 20 November 2024. Each row also says what the control leaves out, because a mapping that only lists matches reads like compliance, and nothing here makes anything compliant.

## Who the regulation binds

The CRA binds the **manufacturer**: "a natural or legal person who develops or manufactures products with digital elements ... and markets them under its name or trademark, whether for payment, monetisation or free of charge" (Article 3, point 13). It applies to products made available on the market, which Article 3, point 22, defines as supply "in the course of a commercial activity". This repository is a free reference that nobody supplies commercially, so it is not such a product.

It is useful to two kinds of reader who are bound:

- **A team that ships container images in a product.** The publish workflow is a working example of producing the evidence Annex I asks for: a signature, provenance, an SBOM and a scan for every image, from the build itself.
- **A team that runs images it did not build.** Article 13(5) asks manufacturers to "exercise due diligence when integrating components sourced from third parties so that those components do not compromise the cybersecurity of the product". The admission policy is that due diligence, enforced: an image runs only with a signer you named, provenance from the repository you expect, an SBOM and a scan that found nothing critical.

## What a team takes from this

The controls here produce the evidence for Article 13(5) and parts of Annex I, and they produce it in the build and at admission, every day, without a person in the loop. That is the part of the regulation a pipeline can carry. Disclosure, reporting within 24 hours, the support period and the conformity assessment are processes a company runs. A pipeline cannot carry them.

## How to explain this to a customer

The Cyber Resilience Act asks two kinds of thing. Some are facts about the software, like which components are inside, who built them, whether they carry known critical flaws and whether anyone tampered with them. A pipeline can record those facts and a cluster can refuse anything that arrives without them. This repository does both, every day, and shows each refusal working. The rest is what your company does when something goes wrong: who answers a vulnerability report, who warns ENISA within 24 hours, how long you support each version. No tool does that for you. What the tooling does is give the people doing it the evidence they need.
