# 3. Provenance, an SBOM, and a scan that read something

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-13

## Context

A signature says who built an image. It does not say from what, what is inside, or whether any of it is known to be exploitable today.

## Decision

The publish workflow attaches three signed attestations to every image it signs, and the policy requires all three:

- SLSA v1 provenance whose resolved source is this repository, generated and signed in the build job;
- a CycloneDX SBOM from Syft;
- a vulnerability scan from Trivy in the cosign-vuln format, which must contain an operating system's packages and no critical finding.

The scan is re-run and re-attested daily on a schedule.

## Consequences

- An image with a critical vulnerability is refused even though it is signed and has provenance; the e2e run proves it with one.
- A scan that read nothing is refused instead of counted as clean. Building on a base the scanner recognises is part of the contract.
- The provenance is Build L2: written by the job that builds. An isolated generator would be L3 and is the upgrade path.
- Older scans stay attached to the image; see the threat model.

## Alternatives

- **Scan at admission time.** Always current, and slow and network-heavy on the admission path; a scan result signed by the pipeline is cheaper to check.
- **SBOM only, no scan gate.** Inventory without a decision; it tells you what is inside and leaves the question of whether it may run to a person.

## How to explain this to a customer

Each piece of software comes with a signed record of where it was built, a list of what is inside it, and a security scan from within the last day. Anything with a known critical weakness is stopped before it starts, even if our own pipeline built it.

## 2026-10-27: asked of Trivy

The check this record adds was asked of Trivy itself, in [aquasecurity/trivy Discussion #11403](https://github.com/aquasecurity/trivy/discussions/11403) on 2026-10-13: a warning when no operating system is detected, an option to fail when nothing was scanned, or a field in the report that says whether OS packages were read. A maintainer answered on 2026-10-27 that Trivy does not plan any of them. An image with no detected OS is expected for many targets, a `scratch` image with one binary among them, so a warning would rest on heuristics that miss some cases or warn on others. The answer named checking for `Metadata.OS` and a result of class `os-pkgs`, the second of which `policies/verify-images.yaml` requires, as the most reliable way to know the OS packages were scanned; Trivy's own trace of the miss is the line `OS is not detected.`, under `--debug` only. So the requirement stays in the policy: no signal from the scanner will replace it.

## 2026-10-27: Build L1, not L2

The consequences above call the provenance Build L2. By the text of SLSA v1.0 it is Build L1, and the line above is left as it was written. [Provenance is Authentic](https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.0/requirements#provenance-authentic), required from L2, asks that the provenance be generated by the build platform's control plane, not by a tenant of the platform, with the names and digests of the outputs as the one exception, and that the platform keep tenants from tampering with it. Here every field is written by a step of the publish job (`.github/workflows/publish.yml`) and signed with that job's keyless identity, so a step of the build could write anything into it.

What admission proves is unchanged. The signature still names the publish workflow on main, from GitHub's own token, which no step of the job can change, and the policy accepts no other signer (ADR 2). What the provenance's fields say, the source repository the policy reads among them, is the build job's word. Provenance written by the build platform, or by an isolated generator, is the upgrade path, to L2 and to L3. Found while mapping this account's repositories to SLSA v1.0's Build track.
